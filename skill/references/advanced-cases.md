# Solid case v2 additions

V2 retains strict supports, criteria, mesh and physical-condition fields. Supported model formats and semantic interfaces are described in [model-adapters.md](model-adapters.md). Constitutive support includes isotropic elasticity with an applicable von Mises yield criterion and calibrated orthotropic elasticity with signed material-axis maximum-stress allowables; see [materials.md](materials.md) for parameters, calibration and applicability. Explicit independent-body assemblies use the separate `assembly-1.0` contract and qualified planar connections described in [assemblies.md](assemblies.md). These are implemented capabilities within their stated qualification boundaries.

Loads add `total_moment_n_mm`, optional `application_point_mm` and boolean `scalable` (default true). A couple is a moment at the stated application point, or at the selected patch centroid if no point is supplied. The backend distributes surface traction plus a least-norm nodal force couple, preserving all three force and moment components. This is an ideal wrench transfer through a declared full surface, not actual bearing/bolt/contact stress. Zero force with a nonzero couple is valid. Multiple v2 loads may superpose on the same surface; a surface that is also fixed remains invalid.

`gravity: {acceleration_m_s2:[gx,gy,gz]}` requires positive `material.density_kg_m3`. Gravity is integrated as consistent quadratic volume loads in mm/N/MPa units. Quadratic corner-node weights may be negative and are not clipped. Gravity and `scalable:false` loads remain fixed while payloads vary. Self-weight-only cases may have `loads: []`, and never invent a payload capacity.

The reference and fixed-load subcases use the same mesh/material/supports. Their fields combine as `response(alpha) = response_fixed + alpha*(response_reference-response_fixed)`. Capacity search enforces peak integration-point von Mises stress and nodal displacement at the candidate alpha, and checks convergence/small-response screening there too. Fixed loads that already fail suppress extrapolation. Raw CalculiX `RF` contains reaction plus applied loads; since this backend writes CLOAD, the loads at constrained nodes are subtracted when recovering reaction force/moment.

`buckling: {enabled:true, modes:3, safety_factor:2, eigenvalue_relative_tolerance:0.05}` invokes real CalculiX elastic eigenvalue buckling at each mesh. A fixed preload is solved first; eigenfactors apply to the scalable pattern about that preload. The lowest positive factor must converge over the last two refinements. Requested normalized mode vectors are saved and may be viewed with arbitrary display amplification. Negative factors represent reversed loading; no positive factor is unresolved evidence, not proof of stability. The safety factor is a declared screening margin, not a calibrated imperfection factor.

Eigenvalue buckling omits nonlinear collapse, geometric imperfections, plasticity, contact changes and manufacturing effects. A finite number of converged modes on a resolved mesh cannot establish arbitrary unsearched instability mechanisms. Keep these limitations and all component omissions with a capacity signal.

`load_cases` is a nonempty list of `{name, loads, gravity?}` alternative operating cases. Names must be unique nonempty strings. Every child runs all its own numerical and conditional-limit checks; the envelope takes the worst status and, only if every limit is available, the smallest common scalable-load factor. It does not apply all alternative loads simultaneously.

`provenance` records generator, model/revision, parameters, task ID and source file. No material or physical-condition verification is inferred from generator metadata. See the [validation scope for this checkout](../validation/README.md) for the included evidence and the status of archived records. These records concern numerical/software verification, not real-product load testing; they do not grant fresh full numerical qualification to edited sources.
